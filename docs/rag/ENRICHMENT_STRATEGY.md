# Model Selection Strategy for Enrichment

This document details the strategy deployed to optimize the Cost/Quality/Latency ratio of the chunk enrichment pipeline. 

This strategy is based on empirical benchmarks performed on the **Ministral 3B** (Light) vs **Ministral 8B** (Strong) models.

---

## 1. Context & Objective

The enrichment step transforms raw text chunks into structured metadata (summary, intents, emotions, entities). This is the most computationally expensive part of indexing.

- **Ministral 8B**: High quality (Score ~0.89), but slow (~14.5s/chunk).
- **Ministral 3B**: Very fast (~4.5s/chunk), but fails on complex tasks (Score ~0.09 on Medium/Complex inputs).

**Goal:** Use the 3B model *only* where it is reliable to save time, and route everything else to the 8B model to ensure data integrity.

---

## 2. Benchmark Findings

Our evaluation revealed a critical performance cliff for the 3B model:

| Complexity Category | Score 8B | Score 3B | Observation |
|---------------------|----------|----------|-------------|
| **Simple** (< 0.25) | 0.86     | **0.84** | 3B is nearly as good as 8B, but 3x faster. |
| **Medium** (0.25-0.40) | 0.89 | **0.56** | **Danger Zone**. 3B collapses (empty JSON fields). |
| **Complex** (> 0.40)   | 0.87 | 0.63     | 3B is overwhelmed by context density. |

**Key Insight:** The 3B model is "scared" of constraints in the system prompt. When faced with implicit information (intentions, emotions) in medium-complexity text, it prefers to return empty lists rather than risk hallucination.

---

## 3. Implementation: Strict Complexity Routing

We implemented a router based on `ChunkComplexityAnalyzer` with strict thresholds to protect the index quality.

### A. Complexity Scoring
Chunks are scored (0.0 - 1.0) based on 6 weighted metrics:

- **Participants (20%)**: Baseline score of **0.3** for DMs (2 people) to fix bias against private convos.
- **Information Content (40%)**: Volume of unique concepts (Unique Words). Replaces Density & Lexical Diversity.
- **Size (20%)**: Number of messages.
- **Dialogue (15%)**: Presence of questions, exclamations, emojis (proxy for emotional complexity).
- **Media (5%)**: Attachments and links.

### B. Routing Logic

| Range         | Label     | Targeted Model | Rationale |
|---------------|-----------|----------------|-----------|
| **0.00 - 0.25** | **Simple** | **Ministral 3B** | Phatic exchanges, reactions, short monologues. Safe for 3B. |
| **0.25 - 0.40** | **Medium** | **Ministral 8B** | *Changed from Light to Strong.* Contains implicit social cues and intents that 3B misses. |
| **0.40 - 1.00** | **Complex** | **Ministral 8B** | Dense narratives, conflicts, planning. Requires full 8B reasoning. |

### C. Batching Strategy
To avoid "model ping-pong" (loading/unloading latency), processing is grouped:
1. Load a batch of chunks (e.g., 20 items).
2. Classify all chunks.
3. Process all **Simple** chunks with 3B.
4. Switch model (once).
5. Process all **Medium + Complex** chunks with 8B.
6. Save and iterate.

---

## 4. Performance Impact

- **Quality**: Guaranteed >0.80 average validity score across the index. We eliminated the "Zero-Information" chunks generated by 3B on medium inputs.
- **Speed**: We offload approx. 30-40% of the easiest chunks to the fast model, saving ~10s per chunk on that segment.
- **Cost**: Reduced computational load on simple, high-frequency "chitchat" chunks.

## 5. Future Improvements

- **Fine-tuning 3B**: A LoRA fine-tune on the 3B model could help it become less "timid" on Medium chunks.
- **Speculative Decoding**: Using 3B as a drafter for 8B could speed up the Medium segment (requires MLX/llama.cpp support).
